🤔 What makes a theme?

A theme is an arguable idea about human nature or society that a story develops. It can apply beyond the characters, place, and time of that story. Different interpretations can work when evidence supports them.

📖 Read These Four Micro-Stories

You will be assigned one of these stories to analyze through all seven stages of Theme Studio. Read all four to understand your options.

Story 1: The Garden

Someone discovered an old, overgrown garden behind the library. They decided to restore it, planting new flowers and clearing weeds. Neighbors began stopping by to help and to sit among the plants. When the library announced budget cuts that would close the garden, the community protested. The garden remained open.

Story 2: The Challenge

Someone tried out for a challenging team, though they had never done it before. During tryouts, they froze during their opening performance. They tried again the next year and stumbled over their words again. A friend told them to give up, that some people were not suited for this. They joined anyway. They started with easier rounds and learned from feedback. By competition season, they won three regional awards.

Story 3: The Question

In class, someone raised their hand to disagree with the teacher's interpretation of a poem. The teacher seemed annoyed at first. The student explained their reasoning carefully, using lines from the text. The teacher paused and then said, "That's a fair point. I hadn't considered that." Other students started sharing their own questions after that.

Story 4: The Offer

Someone was offered a chance to make money quickly, no experience needed. The job involved recruiting others into a sales scheme. They were tempted by the promise of cash. They asked their parents about it first. Their parents explained why the scheme was unsustainable and unfair. They declined, though their friend was disappointed.

📌 Topic → thematic statement

Topic: friendship

A topic is a subject. A theme says something meaningful about that subject.

✔️ Keep these distinctions

Plot summary: what happens.
Main idea: the central point of a text or passage.
Moral: a lesson about behavior.
Theme: an interpretation supported by the story.

A moral can overlap with theme. For this assignment, write an observation about people rather than advice. Use confident, precise language; "always" and "will" are not required.

📚 Practice: Learn the Three Categories

Read these worked examples to see how Topic, Main Idea, and Theme are different:

TOPIC

"Growing up"

← A subject. One or two words.

MAIN IDEA

"The character realizes adulthood requires sacrifice."

← The central point of THIS story.

THEME

"Maturity arrives when people accept loss as the price of independence."

← A universal idea ANY story could explore.

Why the difference matters: A main idea is about THIS character. A theme is about PEOPLE IN GENERAL. You can find the same theme in many different stories.
